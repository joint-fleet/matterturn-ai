# A-03 — When the Right Answer Hides a Bad Process

**Tags:** Failure Preserved · Engineering Validation

## The Question

A real-world residential acquisition analyst exercise, worked without network access, was later checked against what actually happened. The final direction turned out to be protective — but was the path that got there actually sound?

## What the system was given

A frozen participant package for a real residential acquisition, examined before the outcome was known.

## What it found

Several issues that later proved materially relevant: dependence on rent-regulation conversion assumptions, thin current debt coverage, reliance on reserves during stabilization, and the structural importance of mezzanine debt that headline senior-loan coverage ratios didn't capture.

## The judgment / result

The qualitative recommendation was directionally protective — the acquisition and capital structure it flagged concerns about did go on to fail.

## What remained blocked, unresolved, or preserved

The run could not be certified as clean predictive validation, for reasons kept on record rather than smoothed over: the analysis recognized the historical asset mid-process and could not be proven independent of hindsight on regulatory risk; the formal valuation path used invented or unsupported lease terms to fit the model, generating false-precision outputs (exact price ceilings, NPV, IRR) that outcome agreement does not rehabilitate; the source package itself omitted a legal dependency that later became central; and one key financial figure's time basis was left ambiguous. Four separate failure classes, kept distinct rather than collapsed into "the model was right" or "the model was wrong."

## What this case demonstrates

Being right about the outcome doesn't certify the reasoning, and when apparently precise analysis turns out to be unsupported, this system is built to say so and assign the failure to the correct layer — identity contamination, model-input failure, research-coverage gap, or definition error — rather than hide it behind a correct-looking conclusion.
